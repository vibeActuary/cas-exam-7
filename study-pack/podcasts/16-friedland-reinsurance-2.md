# Episode 16 — Friedland, ceded reserve methods

**Hosts:** Riley and Sam  
**Length:** about 18 minutes  
**Tasks:** A15, A16  
**Paper:** Friedland 2022 study note

---

**Riley:** I am the cedent. I bought a tower. How do I book ceded unpaid?

**Sam:** Often the best estimate is a function of gross unpaid and the program terms, not an independent ceded chain ladder. Work policy-by-policy or segment-by-segment through the tower.

Watch the basis. Ground-up versus limited versus excess — episodes 05 and 06. ALAE pro-rata or in addition. Whether the gross ultimate already includes events that do not recover: below retention, excluded perils, exhausted limits. Reinstatement premium due if the layer is expected to burn — that is a premium liability, A5-adjacent.

Net unpaid equals gross unpaid minus ceded unpaid. If your ceded-triangle method and your gross method disagree, do not book a net that is smaller than a plausible retention. That sentence has paid exam points for decades.

**Riley:** I am the reinsurer. I do not have the primary’s triangle.

**Sam:** Then you do not run Mack on eight thin years and call it a day. Toolkit:

Expected loss ratio, BF, or Cape Cod on on-level treaty premium, with a pattern that is slower than primary.

Exposure rating: limits profile times ILFs times a ground-up rate, for the layer, then BF the IBNR.

Experience rating if you actually have enough years on a stable structure.

Bornhuetter-Ferguson on reported-to-date with a reinsurer lag pattern.

Cape Cod across a portfolio of similar treaties — Hürlimann’s collective prior.

Stochastic only after you have a mean you believe. A Mack SE on eight thin years is theater.

**Riley:** Excess assumed, specifically.

**Sam:** Frequency of layer hits times severity in the layer. Development of frequency — late reports into the layer — is often more important than severity development. That is Siewert and Sahasrabuddhe again.

**Riley:** Cat.

**Sam:** Scenario or model residual, plus IBNER on known events. Do not average three cats in a triangle and call it a pattern. Three points is not a development factor.

**Riley:** Aggregates and commutations.

**Sam:** A commutation settles unpaid. After it, those liabilities are gone. Any triangle that still contains them must be restated. That is A1 for reinsurance.

An aggregate deductible or corridor: ceded unpaid is near zero until the subject ultimate approaches the attachment, then it ramps fast. That is a nonlinear function of the primary ultimate. Another reason not to develop ceded dollars independently. Think of ceded IBNR as an option on the subject ultimate.

**Riley:** Quota share reasonableness.

**Sam:** Ceded over gross should sit near the cession percent, after corridors and commission effects. If you booked forty percent ceded on a thirty percent quota share, you owe a sentence about a corridor, a cap, or a mistake.

Recoveries to date plus ceded unpaid, versus remaining limit. You cannot recover more than the limit, including reinstatements if they are bought.

For a working excess: implied hit counts versus the size model.

A new accident year with zero reported and a huge quota-share IBNR can be fine — the percent still applies to a BF gross. A new accident year with zero reported and a huge *cat-layer* IBNR needs a scenario, not an LDF.

**Riley:** Reinstatement premium.

**Sam:** If the layer is burning, you may owe another premium to put the limit back. That cashflow is part of the reinsurance result. Forgetting it is the retro-premium-asset mistake in a different jacket.

**Riley:** Exam grab bag.

**Sam:** Cedent: push gross unpaid through the contract. Reinsurer: ELR, BF, Cape Cod, slower lag. Excess: frequency times severity-in-layer, not a six-year excess CL. Aggregate cover: option on the subject ultimate. Open burning layer: add reinstatement premium. Net cannot be smaller than a plausible retention if gross and ceded methods disagree.

**Riley:** Repeat cedent versus reinsurer.

**Sam:** Cedent has the gross triangle and the contract — push through. Reinsurer has premium, a lag, and maybe a bordereau — BF and exposure, not Mack theater.
