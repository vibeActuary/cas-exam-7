# Episode 00 — Exam map

**Hosts:** Riley (sitting October 2026) and Sam (has the outline cold)  
**Length:** about 15 minutes  
**Tasks:** none — this is the map

---

**Sam:** You are on a plane, and this is CAS Exam 7. Official name: Advanced Estimation of Claims Liabilities. One domain. One hundred percent of the exam. October window is the nineteenth through the twenty-seventh, twenty twenty-six. Appointment is four and a half hours. You get four hours and fifteen minutes of items and a fifteen-minute break.

**Riley:** I keep calling it “reserving.” Is that wrong?

**Sam:** It is the reserving exam. The outline is more specific. You estimate unpaid claims, you test them, you put a distribution around them, you put a range and a risk margin on them, and you do the same job when the contract is reinsurance. If you can only do chain ladder on a pretty primary triangle, you are not done.

**Riley:** Seventeen tasks. Do I memorize the numbers?

**Sam:** Yes. They show up as A1 through A17 in the text-reference table, and they are how this whole series is built.

A1 is data diagnostics. Dirty triangle, changing mix, a coded claim that owns a year. You fix that before you fit anything.

A2 through A5 are point estimates. Calculate unpaid. Test unpaid. Do it by layer. And — this surprises people — forecast premium reserves, meaning the retro premium asset.

A6 through A10 are the stochastic block. Parameters, moments and percentiles, simulation, mean and prediction error, predictive distributions.

A11 through A14 are “now look at the output.” Test the distribution, test the assumptions, build a range, calculate a risk margin.

A15 through A17 are reinsurance. Adjust primary methods, calculate ceded reserves, describe the function and types.

**Riley:** Cognitive mix?

**Sam:** Almost none of it is “remember the year Mack published.” Forty to fifty percent understand-and-apply. Forty to fifty percent analyze-and-evaluate. A little create. That is why a correct formula with the wrong story loses points, and a good story with a named method and a number keeps them.

**Riley:** Readings. How many, and what changed for twenty twenty-six?

**Sam:** Fifteen citations. All marked OP — online publications. You do not need a paid study kit for the required papers. Three version traps.

One: Meyers is Monograph number eight, second edition. Not Monograph one.

Two: Taylor and McGuire is Monograph three, chapters one through six. Not one through three.

Three: Hürlimann, you are not responsible for the proofs. Teng and Perkins, skip section five, and from Feldblum’s discussion take sections one and two only. No Annual Statement line numbers.

**Riley:** Give me the cast of papers the way I should hear them in my head.

**Sam:** Credibility family: Brosius, Mack Benktander, Hürlimann. They all mix a data estimate with a prior. Different knobs.

Curve-fitting and exposure: Clark. Weibull or loglogistic growth curve, maximum likelihood, Cape Cod versus free ultimates.

Layers: Sahasrabuddhe for the algebra that ties trend, size model, and LDFs together. Siewert for workers-comp high deductibles, which is that algebra with WC dirt on it.

Premium asset: Teng and Perkins plus Feldblum. Losses develop, retro premium develops. If you book one and not the other, you moved surplus for no reason.

Variance of chain ladder: Mack nineteen ninety-four. Three assumptions, process plus parameter, do not add the year standard errors.

Assumption police: Venter. Residual plots. Intercept test. Calendar-year diagonal.

GLM brain: Taylor. Chain ladder is a GLM. Once you believe that, you can test it and leave it.

Bootstrap brain: Shapland. ODP residual bootstrap, and a monograph of reasons the textbook recipe breaks.

Bayesian brain: Meyers eight. The CAS outcome database failed Mack and the ODP bootstrap more often than people admit. Changing settlement rate for paid. Correlated accident years for incurred. Cost-of-capital risk margin.

Expert inside the distribution: Verrall.

Risk-margin governance: Marshall. The triangle does not contain legislative risk. Stop pretending a Mack seventy-fifth is a risk margin.

Reinsurance textbook: Friedland. Types, why primary methods break, how a cedent versus a reinsurer actually books unpaid.

You are not studying “reserving in general.” You are studying unpaid claims, their distributions, their margins, and reinsurance.
