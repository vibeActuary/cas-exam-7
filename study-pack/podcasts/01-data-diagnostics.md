# Episode 01 — Dirty triangles

**Hosts:** Riley and Sam  
**Length:** about 16 minutes  
**Tasks:** A1  
**Papers in the background:** Venter, Shapland, Brosius, Friedland

---

**Riley:** A1 says “perform data diagnostic analyses and adjust for data issues.” That sounds like a warmup. Is it a warmup?

**Sam:** It is the first task because every later task inherits it. Mack’s standard error on a triangle that has a calendar-year shock is a precise number attached to the wrong object. Shapland will tell you a single miscoded claim can own the ninety-ninth percentile. Friedland will tell you last year’s five-xs-five and this year’s ten-xs-ten are not one triangle.

**Riley:** What am I actually looking at when they hand me a triangle?

**Sam:** Paid or incurred, and you say which before you breathe. Then: is it ground-up, limited, excess, loss, or loss-and-ALAE? Then: accident year, report year, treaty year, policy year? Then: are the ages twelve, twenty-four, thirty-six, or are they fiscal? If you get the basis wrong, A4 and A16 are automatic zeros even if the algebra is pretty.

**Riley:** Walk the diagnostics I can do without a model.

**Sam:** One. Volume. Premium, earned exposure, claim counts, average severity, by year. A year that is one-third the size of the others does not get a full-credibility LDF. That is Brosius’s whole personality.

Two. Incrementals. Look at the incremental triangle, not just the cumulatives. A negative paid incremental is a recovery or a coding flip. A negative incurred incremental is a case-reserve take-down. Neither is “the chain ladder is broken” by itself. Both are “do not blindly take logs.”

Three. The latest diagonal. If every year on the latest diagonal is high, you do not have seventeen independent accident years. You have a calendar-year event: inflation, statute, settlement speed-up, a new claims system. Venter’s calendar residual plot is this test.

Four. One cell that is a moon. Open it. If it is one claimant, you treat it as a large-loss, not as a development factor.

Five. Mix. Limit profile, deductible mix, state mix, medical-only versus lost-time. If the mix moved, the LDF moved, and Sahasrabuddhe is waiting for you in episode 05.

**Riley:** What does “adjust” mean? They want me to rewrite the triangle?

**Sam:** Sometimes. Adjustments that are legal on this exam:

Restate history onto a common limit and a common cost level before you pick factors. That is Sahasrabuddhe’s equation three-point-two.

Pull a large loss out, develop the attritional book, put the large loss back with its own story.

On-level the premium if you are about to do Cape Cod or BF, so the ELR is not a mix of rate-inadequate years and rate-adequate years.

For reinsurance, restate every year onto the current attachment and limit, or do not put them in the same triangle.

What is not an adjustment: quietly dropping the latest year because it is inconvenient.

**Riley:** Paid versus incurred. Which diagnostics differ?

**Sam:** Paid is honest about cash and lying about speed. If settlement sped up, paid LDFs shrink and you under-reserve. Meyers built a whole model for that — changing settlement rate.

Incurred is smoother because humans already put case reserves in it. That smoothness makes Mack look great in-sample and then fail when the next year of outcomes arrives. Meyers documented that on the CAS database. If all recent incurred years strengthened together, that is correlated accident years, not fourteen independent Mack years.

**Riley:** Counts.

**Sam:** If you have counts, use them. Reported counts tell you whether the dollar movement is frequency or severity. A year with normal counts and a huge average is a large-loss year. A year with low counts and a normal average is a volume year. Brosius credibility cares. Siewert’s deductible layer cares. A reinsurer’s excess frequency cares.

**Riley:** They give me a residual plot in a point-and-click item.

**Sam:** Click the latest diagonal if it is a stripe of the same sign. Click the early ages if the fan opens — variance function is wrong. Click the huge year if one point owns the regression. Do not click the pretty 1.05 factor just because it is a number.

**Riley:** Exam grab bag.

**Sam:** Say the basis first. Look at incrementals, volumes, the latest diagonal, and the moon-cell. Adjust by restating, isolating large losses, or on-leveling — not by deleting. Paid hides settlement speed. Incurred hides correlated case-reserve judgment. If A1 is wrong, A6 through A14 are theater.

**Riley:** Repeat the four looks.

**Sam:** Volume. Incrementals. Latest diagonal. Moon-cell. Then mix.

**Riley:** Next episode, Brosius actually gives us a formula for the thin year.
