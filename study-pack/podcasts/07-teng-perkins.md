# Episode 07 — Retro premium asset

**Hosts:** Riley and Sam  
**Length:** about 16 minutes  
**Tasks:** A5  
**Papers:** Teng & Perkins 1996 (no section 5); Feldblum 1998 sections 1–2 only

---

**Riley:** We booked more loss reserve. The CFO asks why surplus just died. Sometimes the answer is: you forgot the premium asset.

**Sam:** On a retrospectively rated policy, premium is a function of losses. After the policy period you still book additional or return premium as losses develop. If you have an unpaid-loss reserve and you have not booked the extra retro premium those losses will generate, the balance sheet is missing an asset. If the development will generate return premium, you are missing a liability. A5 is: forecast that premium reserve, consistent with the loss reserve.

**Riley:** Linear retro in words.

**Sam:** Premium equals basic premium plus conversion factor times loss, the whole thing times the tax multiplier, then subject to a minimum and a maximum.

If the policy is not at the min or max, an extra dollar of loss creates conversion-factor times tax-multiplier dollars of premium. Say it: dP equals CF times TM times dL.

If the policy is already at the maximum, dP is zero. Future loss development does not make more premium.

If the policy is at the minimum, additional losses may produce no premium until you climb off the min. The first dollars of development eat the cushion.

**Riley:** Repeat the open-policy formula.

**Sam:** dP equals CF times TM times dL. Book-wide, the incremental ratio lives between zero and CF times TM, not equal to it for everyone.

**Riley:** Teng and Perkins versus policy-by-policy.

**Sam:** The accurate way: take the selected ultimate loss for each policy, run the retro formula, subtract premium already booked. Expensive.

The triangle way: look at how booked retro premium has historically moved as losses developed. Build something like an additional-premium-to-loss ratio, apply it to current unpaid losses, and haircut for the fraction of the book at max, the fraction at min, and any change in plan parameters — CF, TM, loss limits.

**Riley:** Feldblum sections one and two. Why did they add a discussion?

**Sam:** Because the economics got lost in statutory choreography. You are not responsible for Annual Statement notation. You are responsible for this:

The premium asset is expected additional premium arising from loss development and from the retro formula. It is not a plug to pretty up the combined ratio.

It must be consistent with the loss reserve. Raise the loss reserve by ten million on a fully open book, and you raise the premium asset by about CF times TM times ten million. Do one and not the other, and P&L and surplus move the wrong way.

Min, max, loss limitations, and the insurance charge mean dP over dL is between zero and CF times TM.

You also owe an asset or liability for future additional or return premium on losses already booked if the last adjustment has not been issued. Not only for IBNR.

**Riley:** Out of syllabus.

**Sam:** Teng and Perkins section five. Line numbers on the blank. Schedule P mapping. Do not volunteer them.

**Riley:** Trick ending.

**Sam:** “All policies are at the maximum.” The premium asset on *future* loss development is zero. Write zero, and write why.

**Riley:** Exam grab bag.

**Sam:** Define the premium asset in one sentence. Compute dP equals CF times TM times dL, then haircut for the max probability. If they increase the loss reserve, say what happens to the premium asset with a min/max split, not a single macho number. Same loss ultimate, same definition of loss — limited? ALAE in? — for both sides.

**Riley:** Repeat the consistency sentence.

**Sam:** Loss reserve and premium asset use the same loss ultimate. If you move one, you move the other, unless the book is at the max.
